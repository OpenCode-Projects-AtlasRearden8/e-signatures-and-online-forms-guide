Chapter 3: Signing a PDF on Your Computer

You have a PDF that needs your signature. Here are the tall-free trees to shake: Windows, Mac, and Linux, using only built-in or genuinely free tools.

The universal no-account method (any computer)

Open the PDF in your browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari — click the file, or drag it into a browser window). Modern browsers can add text boxes and draw a signature directly on the PDF, then let you print or save the completed file.

  • Edge (Windows): open the PDF → toolbar Draw and Add text → save with Save as.
  • Chrome: open the PDF → use the annotate tools (text/draw) → save via print → “Save as PDF.”
  • Firefox: open → use the built-in text highlighting and drawing tools → download the edited copy.

Best for: form fills and quick draws when you do not want to install anything at all. Limitation: no saved signature image and no tamper-evidence certificate.

The best free method: signing tools on each platform

Windows: Adobe Acrobat Reader (free)

Adobe’s free Reader is safe when downloaded from Adobe’s own site (adobe.com/acrobat/pdf-reader). Steps:

  1. Open your PDF in Acrobat Reader.
  2. Click Fill & Sign in the right-hand toolbar (or Tools → Fill & Sign).
  3. Click Sign yourself → Add signature.
  4. Choose: Type (pick a handwriting-ish font), Draw (mouse or trackpad’s stiff finger mode), or Image (upload your signature photo from Chapter 2).
  5. Click once on the PDF where you want it; drag to size; it now behaves like form content.
  6. To fill text fields, click Fill & Sign → Add text, then click fields and type.
  7. File → Save (yes, keep the same format; no “export” step needed).

Optional upgrade — a certificate-based signature (tamper-evident, free, two minutes):

  1. Tools → Use a certificate → Digitally sign.
  2. When asked for a Digital ID, choose “Configure a new Digital ID,” then select “Create a new Digital ID.” As the destination, pick “Save to File” (the simple option, not the more exotic ones).
  3. Fill in just a name and email (use your real name and your normal email). Set a password you will remember.
  4. Now “Digitally sign” draws a box on the PDF; when you save, the file locks: Acrobat shows a green check and the text “document has not been modified since it was signed,” which is exactly the sentence you want a landlord or a court to see.

Get the sibling PDF Everyday Handbook if you need heavy editing beyond signing.

Mac: Preview (already installed, best in class)

  1. Open the PDF in Preview (double-click, or right-click → Open With → Preview).
  2. Click the Markup pencil icon; if the toolbar lacks the signature button, View → Customize Toolbar → drag Sign onto it.
  3. Click Sign → Create Signature (first time only). Choose: - Trackpad: sign with your finger on the trackpad. - Camera: hold your paper-signed name up to the webcam on the guide line — Preview grabs it, straightens, and makes the background transparent. This one is magic and takes 20 seconds.
  4. Once created, click Sign → your signature → click on the page to place it.
  5. Use text boxes (Markup “T”) for typed fields.
  6. File → Save or Export.

Preview saves your signatures for reuse across all documents on that Mac — no account, no cloud, stays on your Mac.

Linux

  • Okular (KDE): open → Tools → Annotations → freehand/text signatures.
  • Xournal++: excellent for hand-drawn placement; export final PDF.
  • Adobe Acrobat Reader also exists as a snap/flatpak on many distros and supports the digital-certificate flow described above.

Which method, one-line answers

You need… Easiest free answer
Type my name on one form Your browser’s Add Text; or Acrobat Reader Fill & Sign
Draw with finger/mouse Mac Preview trackpad; Acrobat Reader Draw; Xournal++
My real paper signature on the file Camera capture in Preview, or signature image (Chapter 2)
Tamper-proof signature box Acrobat Reader certificate (2 minutes, free)
No account, no uploads of any kind Built-in tools: Preview, Edge, Okular, or browser annotate

When the PDF has real form fields vs when it doesn’t

Some PDFs have actual fillable form fields (gray boxes, tab-jump fields). Great tools for those: Acrobat Reader is reliable at detecting and filling them; browsers usually handle them too.

Some PDFs are just “a printed page turned into a PDF” — no real fields. Then you overlay: add text boxes and signature marks wherever the underline demands it. Every tool above does this. Type, click, place, save. Do not stress about pixel perfection; readability is the bar.

The post-sign checklist (this becomes Chapter 6’s first habit)

  1. Open the saved copy and look at it. Every signature present? Every field filled? Name spelled right? Date right?
  2. Save it where you will look later — naming and filing come in Chapter 6.
  3. If you used the tool request flow (someone sent the request via DocuSign or similar), download the completed PDF with the audit certificate they offer. Do it while you are in the flow; it is the receipt for the whole event.
  4. Email it to yourself as the low-tech second copy, especially on a shared or borrowed computer.
