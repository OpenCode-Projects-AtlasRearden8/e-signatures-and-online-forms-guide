Introduction: Going Paperless, Safely

Welcome. This manual is for one specific journey: replacing pen-and-ink signing with electronic signatures and online forms, without losing sleep over whether any of it is legal, safe, or sane.

You do not need to be technical. You do not need to pay for anything. Every method in this book works with free tools and the phone or computer you already own.

Who this book is for

  • Anyone who has ever been handed a PDF that “just needs a signature”
  • Renters, patients, parents, freelancers, and small-business owners
  • People filling out government, insurance, or school forms online who want to get it right the first time
  • Anyone who worries a printed-out form will end up lost in a drawer

The tone here is the same one Dylan Asdale uses across the Asdale Dynamics handbooks: plain English, step by step, never condescending. If a term like “UETA” shows up, we explain it like a friend would.

What this book covers (and what it does not)

This is the e-signatures and online forms handbook. It covers five things:

  1. Whether your electronic signature is actually legal (Chapter 1)
  2. Free, legitimate places to sign documents online (Chapter 2)
  3. Signing a PDF on your own computer (Chapter 3)
  4. Signing a PDF on your phone or tablet (Chapter 4)
  5. Filling out government and insurance web forms calmly (Chapter 5)
  6. Keeping the signed, “stamped” copies so they prove something later (Chapter 6)
  7. Spotting fake signature requests and e-signature scams (Chapter 7)
  8. Checklists, a glossary, and quick answers (Chapter 8)

Two things it deliberately does not cover:

  • Organizing the paperwork you already have — that is the Digital Papers Handbook (foldering, scanning, naming, backups).
  • The day-to-day mechanics of editing, merging, compressing, or splitting PDFs — that is the PDF Everyday Handbook.

Here we only touch PDF skills when they relate to signing, filling, and saving a proof copy. If you want to split, rotate, or shrink files, read the sibling book.

The safety promise of this book

Every recommendation follows the same rules:

  • Free or already-installed first. Paid tools are mentioned only when free options genuinely cannot do the job.
  • Legitimate sources only. Every tool we name is either built into your device (Apple Preview, Android, iPhone Markup), or is a real, established service. You will never be told to download a mystery “free PDF signer” from a random website.
  • Proof over vibes. Every chapter ends with a saved copy you could show a landlord, an insurer, or a court.
  • No secrets, no personal data. This book is a static document. Nothing here collects your information.

A three-minute mental model

Going paperless safely comes down to four habits that repeat through every chapter:

  1. Verify the sender before you sign. Real signature requests come from real sources (Chapter 1 and Chapter 7).
  2. Sign with a real tool. Built-in device tools or established services, never a lookalike download (Chapters 2–4).
  3. Read before you tap “Complete.” Especially the parts that say you agree to something recurring or legally binding.
  4. Save the stamped copy immediately. The signed PDF is your receipt. Chapter 6 shows how to keep it where you can find it years later.

That is the whole philosophy. Everything else is detail.

How to read this book

  • In a hurry? Read Chapter 8 (Checklists) first, then the chapter that matches your immediate problem.
  • Anxious about a form you must submit today? Go straight to Chapter 5. The “no panic” workflow is designed for exactly that moment.
  • Read straight through? Chapters 1–4 are foundations, Chapter 5–6 are the high-stakes practical chapters, and Chapter 7 is the one that can save you money.

One note before we start: this book explains general legal principles and tool usage. It is educational, not legal advice or medical advice. If a signing decision has serious legal stakes — a will, a deed, a custody agreement — a local attorney or your state’s legal aid office is the right next step.
