Chapter 4: Signing a PDF on Your Phone or Tablet

Phones are where most real-life signing happens — a boss texts a PDF at 9 p.m., the dentist sends a consent form while you sit in the waiting room. Here is how to do it well on an iPhone and on Android, free.

First decision: signed inside the app that sent it, or in your own tool?

If the document arrived as a link in a text or email (“Sign via DocuSign,” “Complete your enrollment”), the smooth road is tap the link and sign inside the sender’s flow. Free for you, copies come back to you automatically. Verify the sender first (Chapter 7) — that is the 80% of phone signing.

If the document arrived as a PDF attachment or a file you downloaded, you sign it yourself with your device’s built-in tools. That is what the rest of this chapter does.

iPhone / iPad: the built-in Markup flow

No app to install. This works in Mail, Messages, Files, Safari, and the Photos- based scans:

  1. Open the PDF (tap it in Mail, Files, or Safari’s download button).
  2. Tap the Markup pencil tip icon (top right on iPhone, or the toolbox icon on iPad).
  3. Tap the + button → Signature.
  4. First time: draw your signature with your finger. Move it to where it belongs; drag corners to size, drag the middle to place.
  5. When the signature dialog closes, keep the saved signature in your list (don’t delete it) — it will be reusable for every future PDF.
  6. Tap + → Text for typed fields; drag each box where its label says (Date, Name, whatever the form asks).
  7. On iPhone the signature picker takes a drawn signature, not an image import — draw it once with your finger and it is saved for every future PDF. (Fancy photo-to-signature capture is a Mac Preview trick; see Chapter 3.)
  8. Tap Done, then use the share icon to save to Files (Chapter 6’s plan) or reply with the signed copy attached.

The floating-signature trick: when the signature dialog is open, you can tap and drag the signature preview around the page while it is still “live” — handy for finicky fields before committing.

The fringe-but-useful photo trick

Screenshot your name written once on white paper; when a tool offers “choose an image,” that screenshot becomes your reusable signature stamp. Zero extra apps. (Details on making this tidy live in Chapter 2, Option A, sibling tricks.)

Android: two built-in paths

Android does not have one universal Markup pane, so pick whichever path your version offers:

Path 1: Google Drive / Google PDF viewer

  1. Open the PDF (Drive opens PDFs natively).
  2. Use the annotate pencil or the overflow menu to add text and freehand drawing.
  3. Save/Share a new copy back into Drive — Drive itself becomes your archive (Chapter 6 expands on this).

Path 2: Adobe Acrobat Reader / Adobe Fill & Sign (free from Play Store)

  1. Open the PDF in Acrobat.
  2. Tap pen icon (annotation) for signature; tap Sign yourself → Add signature; draw, type, or import an image.
  3. The signature is saved on the device for reuse — tap to place it anywhere.
  4. Save the signed copy to your Files/Downloads; done.

Why not “touch-sign by typing” via a web tool on mobile?

It works (Chapter 2’s browser option), but on a phone tab the experience is clunky and you risk a pop-up-ad minefield from search results. Prefer the two paths above. On Android, install-from-Play-Store-only keeps you off the lookalike-app trap (Chapter 7).

Bonus for iPad users: the best “real pen” experience

Apple Pencil + Markup handwriting → tap Done with the handwriting selected → “Convert to text” isn’t for signatures, but the signature flow still works: Markup → signature → draw with pencil → smoother than a finger. That is the nicest free signing experience on any device.

Phone-specific pitfalls (30-second read each)

  • Don’t sign in a screenshot. Take your signature from a legit tool flow, not by pasting a screenshot of someone else’s signature request.
  • Watch the “Done” button. On iPhone, tapping Done locks the markup — if you forget a field, reopen and keep editing; nothing is lost.
  • Where did the file save? Before you share the signed copy, check you know its location (Files app → Recents is the fast way). Chapter 6 gives the naming and filing system so signed copies never float anonymously in Downloads.
  • Borrowed phones: if you signed on a friend’s or work phone, immediately use the share sheet to email the signed PDF to yourself, then the other party. Then clear it from the device if it was private.
  • Dual-use caution for healthcare workers: if you are signing on a work-issued device, remember that your employer can review device activity. Personal documents belong on personal devices.

Phone signing, full loop in one breath

Receive PDF → verify sender → open → Markup/annotate → sign (saved signature) → place → fill text fields → Done → share to Files/Drive/Email → reply to sender with the signed copy → check the sent copy looks right. You are done in five minutes or less. Chapter 6 handles what to do with that saved copy so it stays findable.
