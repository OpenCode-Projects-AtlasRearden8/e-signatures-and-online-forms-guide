# Chapter 8: Checklists, Glossary, and Quick Answers

The whole book compressed into pages you can keep open while you work.

## The master checklist: signing anything electronically

**Before (60 seconds):**
1. Who sent this and why? (expected? context known?)
2. Is the link/button real? (hover / long-press / go to the service directly)
3. Skim the document: money, dates, renewal language
4. Do I have the tool I need? (Chapters 2–4 pick it)

**During:**
5. Fill every field the form asks for; use the format it asks for
6. Sign with a saved signature (fast) or a fresh one (rare, fine)
7. Check what's directly around the signature line

**After (30 seconds):**
8. Open the finished copy and confirm it looks right
9. Save it named `YYYY-MM-DD_kind_who.pdf` into one folder
10. Screenshot any confirmation page with a reference number
11. Email a copy to yourself if it was a shared/borrowed device

## The master checklist: submitting a government or insurance form

1. Personal device, current browser, stable internet, 30+ minutes set aside
2. Documents gathered before opening the portal
3. Support phone number noted before starting
4. Read the page header: what this form does, who signs, deadline
5. Note reference/confirmations as you go
6. 90-second review before Submit (phones/emails/amounts/names)
7. Screenshot confirmation immediately
8. Download any offered PDF confirmation
9. Save all three into your signed folder, per Chapter 6 naming
10. Calendar a follow-up if a response is promised "in X days"

## The scam-spotter's pocket card

Five names for the same rule — **verify out-of-band**:

- Real services: go to them yourself, never through the emailed link alone
- Real senders: confirm a domain you know; call a number you looked up yourself
- Real urgency: legitimate deadlines exist, but never twist your arm in an
  unexpected first contact
- Real copies: a real signature process hands you the completed signed file
- Real tools: Play Store / App Store / built-in device tools — never a site
  that says "click here to download the signer"

## Glossary (plain-English definitions)

**Audit trail / audit certificate** — the extra page(s) a signing service attaches
showing who signed, when, from what device/email. Your independent witness.

**Attribution** — the legal link between a signature and you (your name, session,
email, certificate). One of the three ingredients of a valid signature.

**Countersigned** — signed by the other party after you. Your executed lease comes
back countersigned; save it as the final version.

**Digital certificate / digital ID** — a small encrypted file that makes your
signature mathematically tamper-evident. Created free in Acrobat Reader
(Chapter 3).

**ESIGN Act** — the 2000 US federal law that makes electronic signatures valid.

**Executed copy** — the fully signed final version of an agreement.

**Fillable form fields** — actual boxes inside a PDF where typing works. Contrast
with a "flat" PDF that needs overlays.

**Markup** — Apple's built-in annotation tools; how iPhone/Mac signing works.

**Phishing** — fake messages impersonating real services to steal logins, money,
or identity data.

**Remote online notarization (RON)** — a notary verifies your identity on video
and applies an electronic notarization. Legal in many states and increasingly
common.

**Stamp** — informal word for the signature/date mark placed on a document; the
"stamped copy" is your signed, saved proof.

**UETA** — the model state law (adopted in most US states) that works alongside
ESIGN.

**Wet ink signature** — a traditional pen-and-paper signature. Legally equivalent
to e-signature for most purposes; not intrinsically "more valid."

## Quick answers (FAQ-shaped)

**Q: Is typing my name legally a signature?**
A: Yes (Chapter 1). ESIGN + UETA: intent + attribution beats handwriting shape.

**Q: Do I have to pay for Adobe or DocuSign?**
A: No, not for signing and filling your own documents (Chapter 2). Paying matters
to companies sending documents in bulk, not to you.

**Q: Can I sign while traveling, on my phone?**
A: Yes — Chapter 4's built-in Markup (iPhone) or Adobe Fill & Sign (Android),
offline-friendly and free.

**Q: Is a scanned, signed paper form still valid?**
A: Yes. It's an electronic record of a real signing. Name and store it per
Chapter 6.

**Q: A website asked me to type my name to "e-sign." Is that safe?**
A: It is a legitimate signing method when the site is the real portal of the
company/agency. Verify the portal is real (go there directly, not through an
unexpected email), then screenshot the confirmation (Chapter 5).

**Q: What if the other party says they never got my signed form?**
A: Chapter 6 is your friend: send the copy from your saved folder with a receipt
request, or re-send through the original portal. With reference numbers and
timestamps, resolved in one email.

**Q: Someone emailed me a DocuSign link I don't recognize. What do I do?**
A: Chapter 7: do not click. Open docusign (or whichever service) yourself in a
new tab or app and check whether a real request is waiting. If not, delete.

**Q: Can I sign a document for someone else (my parent, my kid)?**
A: Only if you're authorized to sign on their behalf (power of attorney, guardian
status, or the form explicitly permits a parent/guardian signature). Signing as
yourself "for" them without authority is a legal problem, not a paperwork one.
When in doubt, ask the form-issuer.

**Q: Does my unchanged signature on every PDF matter if my name changes?**
A: Update your saved signature (and certificate identity) after a legal name
change; old documents remain valid with the name that was legal at signing.

**Q: Do I need to notarize e-signatures?**
A: Rarely — only documents whose law requires notarization (certain deeds, affidavits,
powers of attorney). Use remote online notarization where your state permits; ask
the requesting party which method they accept before spending money on a visit.
